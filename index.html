BarbShove

Bet how many of your pieces will cross the board. Not one more, not one fewer. But there are only 4 exits, and they are shared.

You are blue and move up. The rival is red and moves down. The number on each piece is its rank: only a higher rank can push a piece out of the way.

2

You bet first and the rival sees your bet. The rival can't bet 1, because the two bets can't add up to 4, the number of exits.

    How to play

    Each player has 3 pieces, ranked 1 to 3 (the number you see on each one), on a 5×4 board. Two of your pieces start in your front row; the third starts behind them on your back border.

    Shared exits. Each column has one exit, shared by both players: the far row of that column. The first piece to cross there closes the exit for everybody. A closed exit can't be entered. So at most 4 pieces can cross in the whole game. You are fighting over them, just like the tricks in Barbudo.

    Bets. Before the first move you bet how many of your own pieces will cross the board. The rival bets second, and it sees your bet. The two bets can't add up to 4 (the number of exits). Whoever ends closest to their own bet wins. Going over is as bad as falling short. Same distance: tie.

    Your turn. You do one of these two things:

    Advance one piece one step. If the square ahead is empty, you move into it. If it is occupied, you can only enter it if your rank is higher than the piece there. Then you shove ← or →: that piece, and every piece touching it in that direction, slides one square sideways. A piece shoved off the side is lost and counts for nobody. With an equal or lower rank, your piece is blocked.

    Crossing. A piece that steps onto the far row crosses at once: +1 for its owner, and that exit closes. Pieces can't wait on the far row.

    Sidestep (you get just 1 in the whole game): move one of your pieces one square left or right, into an empty square or swapping places with another of your own pieces.

    Pieces never move backward, so the game always ends. If a player has no legal move they pass. If nobody can move, the pieces left on the board are stuck and count for nobody.

    Strategy. Grab an exit before the rival does, or shove their pieces out of the columns they need so they fall short. Or close an exit on purpose so the rival can't reach their bet. Remember: you can't be forced to cross, but the rival can leave you no safe moves.
